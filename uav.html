The brief

The task was to design the wing for a fixed-wing UAV, assessed principally on the range between its minimum and maximum operating speed, and subject to fixed limits on mass and volume. A wide speed range requires a wing that performs at both ends of its envelope: sufficient lift at low speed to remain airborne, and sufficiently low drag at high speed to continue accelerating. Optimising too heavily for either condition compromises the other, or exceeds the mass limit.

Aerodynamic design

I led the aerodynamic configuration work, comparing candidate planforms in XFLR5 and XFOIL and assessing the performance of each across the full speed envelope rather than at a single design point. Flaps and ailerons were used to extend the usable range further, and this was the principal factor separating our wing from the remainder of the year group.

Configuration decisions were made using decision matrices, weighting speed range against stability under load and against manufacturability. Defining the selection criteria before evaluating the options makes the trade-off explicit and produces a design that can be justified afterwards on its merits.

Result

The wing achieved a 23.1 m/s minimum-to-maximum speed range, over 10% greater than any other team in the year group, and the project was ranked 1st in the cohort.

Project management

I also produced the Gantt charts and resource plans used to manage the project. The work was delivered ahead of schedule, which provided time to verify the analysis properly rather than submitting the first result that appeared reasonable.
