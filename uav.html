The brief

Design the wing for a fixed-wing UAV, judged primarily on the range between its minimum and maximum operating speed, and constrained by hard limits on mass and volume. A wide speed range means a wing that works at two ends of its envelope at once: enough lift at low speed to stay airborne, and low enough drag at high speed to keep going. Push either too hard and you lose the other, or you break the mass limit trying.

Aerodynamic design

I led the aerodynamic configuration work, comparing candidate planforms in XFLR5 and XFOIL and assessing how each behaved across the speed envelope rather than at a single design point. Flaps and ailerons were used to extend the usable range further, which is what ultimately separated our wing from the rest of the year.

The decisions were made using decision matrices, weighing speed range against stability under load and against how realistically the thing could be built. Writing the criteria down before choosing matters: it forces the trade-off into the open and produces a design you can justify afterwards, instead of one you merely prefer.

Result

The wing achieved a 23.1 m/s minimum-to-maximum speed range, more than 10% greater than any other team in the year, and the project was ranked 1st in the cohort.

Running the project

I also produced the Gantt charts and resource plans that kept the group on track. The project was delivered ahead of schedule, which was not incidental: finishing early is what created the time to check the analysis properly rather than submitting the first answer that looked reasonable.

What this demonstrates

  • Aerodynamic design against competing objectives and hard physical constraints.
  • Structured, defensible decision-making rather than preference.
  • Leading the technical direction of a group project and planning its delivery.
